# Paper edit

Readers of a paper rarely read it front to back. They read the abstract and introduction, jump to a result, go back to the model for a definition, skip a section. Good structure lets them do that without getting lost: every paragraph does one job, and the text tells readers where they are and where to find things.

This skill is about how the text is organised and written. Whether a claim is too strong or too weak is the job of the `claims-audit` skill.

## Step 1: Map the structure

For the text the user names, list each paragraph's function in a few words, one line per paragraph (e.g., "motivates the friction", "states Proposition 2 in words", "compares to Paper X"). For a whole paper, also give each section's job in one line.

Flag:
- paragraphs with no clear function, or doing two jobs;
- sentences with no role in their paragraph;
- the same point made in more than one place;
- places where a reader arriving from elsewhere would be lost (missing signposts, see below).

## Step 2: Editing rules

- **One function per paragraph.** The first sentence tells the reader what the paragraph does. If a paragraph does two jobs, split it; if two paragraphs do the same job, merge them.
- **Every sentence has a role in its paragraph.** If it does not serve the paragraph's function, cut it or move it where it belongs.
- **Say each thing once,** in the place readers will look for it. The only deliberate repetition is a signpost, such as restating a result in words where it is used.
- **Signposts for non-linear readers:**
  - a roadmap at the end of the introduction;
  - a first sentence in each section saying what the section does and how it connects to the rest;
  - main results restated in words where they are used;
  - explicit cross-references ("Proposition 2", "Section 4", "Assumption 1") instead of "above" or "as shown earlier";
  - key definitions easy to find again, and briefly recalled when used far from where they are defined.
- **Keep the author's voice, notation and claims.** Change how things are said and where, not what is claimed.

## Step 3: Output

- **Review only** (the user asks whether something works): give the structure map with the flagged problems, most important first.
- **Edit:** propose revised text one paragraph or section at a time, each with a one-line note on what changed and why. Apply changes to the files only after the user approves them.
